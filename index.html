Oct 29 Tourmaline All Day

A proper beach day at Tourmaline — the kind where you stop checking the time. Stand up on the bluff and watch it all unfold: surfers lined up on the point, a wide sheet of wet sand running for a mile, and the La Jolla cliffs curving off into the distance.

  • Morning

    Tourmaline Surfing Park

    Free lot off Tourmaline Street, about 100 spaces — roll in before late morning and you're golden. Restrooms and outdoor showers on site.

  • Midday

    Swim, nap, repeat

    That long peaking right-hand point break is the main event — more of a watch-the-surfers, build-a-castle beach than a lap-swimming one. Perfect for us.

  • Golden hour

    Boardwalk stroll

    The cliff-top trail runs the length of the park, up to 75 feet above the water. Best light of the day.

Heads up: no alcohol, glass, or dogs on the sand. Check the surf report that week — fall swell plus a king tide can move a lot of sand around.

Oct 30 Up to the Pines, Out to the Desert

The big day. We leave the coast, climb eight and a half thousand feet into mountain pines before lunch, then drop back down into the desert for boulders, cacti, and a long drink at sundown.

  • Morning

    Palm Springs Aerial Tramway

    The world's largest rotating tram cars. A ten-minute, two-and-a-half-mile climb up the face of Chino Canyon on Mt. San Jacinto — from 2,643 ft on the desert floor to 8,516 ft among the pines. The car turns as it rises, so everyone gets the view: the whole Coachella Valley unrolling below you.

    It's commonly about 20°F cooler up top — mid-80s in the valley, low-60s on the summit. Bring a layer.

  • Lunch

    Yummy sandwiches

    Back down to the valley floor for lunch. Deli counter, cold sandwiches, iced something — exact spot TBD.

  • Afternoon

    Joshua Tree National Park

    October is the start of busy season and the good season — highs around 85°F, cool at night. Skull Rock is a pull-out right off Park Boulevard; Keys View is a twenty-minute drive down for the whole Coachella Valley laid out below you. And if the sky cooperates at golden hour, it lights up in fire — the whole desert goes orange.

    $30 per vehicle, good for seven days. Buy it online and save it to your phone — there's no signal in the park.

  • Dinner

    Tiny Pony Tavern

    Yucca Valley, roughly on the way back from Joshua Tree. An art-covered scratch kitchen and bar — smash burgers, duck nachos, nitro Guinness, a serious cocktail list. Come nightfall the string lights go up and there's usually a skeleton lolling on the roofline. It's been named one of USA Today's best bars in the country, and you'll get why by the first round.

Oct 31 The Sunset Run

Saturday is built backwards, from the sunset. Everything before it — the outdoors, the pizza — is just the warm-up for watching the sun drop straight into the Pacific from a bluff in Bird Rock. Then Halloween, and then the night.

  • Daytime

    Outdoor day

    Whatever the weather's best for — a coastal walk up the boardwalk, tide pools below the bluff, or a long lazy morning with coffee and no plans.

  • Dinner

    Pizza Cassette

    Wood-fired, hand-stretched, charred-crust Neapolitan pies from a scratch kitchen with a retro cassette-tape obsession. You'll know it by the white building, green trim, and the flag out front — order at the counter, grab a lime-green chair, and wait for that leopard-spotted crust.

    The Pacific Beach spot on Garnet does square slices and stays open late — exactly what we want before an evening out.

  • Golden hour

    Sunset at Calumet Park

    A small neighborhood park on the bluff above the Rock Pile, with a nearly 180-degree view from Bird Rock down to False Point. The point peels off in long right-handers and the sun drops straight into the ocean out front. Bring lawn chairs and a blanket.

    No alcohol, glass, or dogs on the beach below. Street parking only, and it's tight.

  • Night

    Halloween pregame → bars

    Back home for costumes, then Pacific Beach and Mission Avenue bar hopping. Halloween night in San Diego is a whole thing — commit to the costume, it pays off.

    First round at Flamingo Deck on Mission Blvd — a lush beach-bungalow bar with glowing cocktails and copper taps, open till 2 AM on Halloween.

Nov 1 Football & a Long Goodbye

The last morning goes the only correct way: coffee, football on the screens, nowhere to be. Then we ruin it by saying goodbye.

  • Breakfast

    Pastries at Wayfarer, coffee at BRCR

    The Sunday ritual starts on Garnet Avenue. Wayfarer for the pastries — flaky, cardamom-dusted, worth the line — then Bird Rock Coffee Roasters next door territory for a proper pour-over to carry to the game.

  • Morning

    Football before we say goodbye

    San Diego lost its team to Los Angeles back in 2016, but it kept the habit — the city still watches Sundays harder than almost anywhere, with a team-specific sports bar for every fanbase that scattered. Early-season kickoffs are the move.

    First pitch is at 10:00 AM Pacific — worth staking out a table before the crowd rolls in.

  • Afternoon

    The goodbye

    Not our favorite part. Bags to the car, hugs at the curb, and the specific silence of a house after people leave.

Good to Know

Time zone

Pacific Daylight all week — except the last morning. DST ends Sun Nov 1 at 2:00 AM, so Sunday is PDT until 2:00 AM and PST after.

Weather

Late October: highs in the upper 60s to mid-70s on the coast, desert afternoons near 85°F. Evenings cool fast — pack a hoodie for the beach and a real jacket for the tram summit.

Book ahead

Tramway tickets are timed and Fridays sell out; Joshua Tree entry is $30 per vehicle, bought online at recreation.gov and saved offline.

Beach rules

No alcohol, glass, or dogs on the sand at Tourmaline and Calumet. Both lots fill early on warm days.
